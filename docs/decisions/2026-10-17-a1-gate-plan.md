# A1 gate plan: the 48-hour run, its fault schedule and its pass conditions

Date: 2026-10-17

Status: proposed. It settles open decision 5 of `2026-10-16-a1-scope.md`. It is to be accepted after the Paper rehearsal (`deploy/pi-host.md` section 9) has run this procedure end to end, and after the record that sets the probe intervals from the storage pilot.

## Context

**Gate A1** (DESIGN.md §13):
- 48 hours continuous on a representative league (NBA, `2026-10-16-a1-league.md`);
- forced faults: a mid-stream socket kill, a process kill, a channel stall, a sequence skip and a resubscribe;
- every fault produces the correct collection gap, or a documented unscoreable interval;
- every resync begins from an authoritative fresh snapshot;
- a storage and depth coverage report.

**The tools exist.**
- **The relay, the control socket and the harness** (`raw_recorder.faults`) cause and label each fault.
- **`clv gate a1`** checks each fault's gaps against the facts, and reports storage and depth.
- **`clv pilot`** reports probe spend, quiet intervals, cutoff verdicts and listing lead over the same period.

**What the rehearsal and the reviews established:**
- **Windows are long and overlap.** A market's capture window runs from `a1.capture_lead_s` (25 h) before its scheduled start to `a1.capture_tail_s` (4 h) after it, so tomorrow's games are subscribed from about this evening.
- **Faults reach beyond their target.**
  - A socket kill or a process kill touches every market subscribed at that moment.
  - A sequence skip or a channel stall drops whole frames, which can carry other markets, tomorrow's included.
- **The gate needs every touched market ingested,** and a market is ingested only once its window has closed and the archive is sealed past it. Stopping early can't be fixed afterwards.
- **Each fault reports how long to keep recording.** It lists its touched markets and when the last of their windows closes (`keep_recording_until_utc`).
- **A fault that touches no market fails its check.** A sequence skip that finds no delta is recorded as not applied, and isn't counted. A channel stall on a quiet market drops nothing, yet reports success (Consequences).

## Decision

### 1. When and how

- **The order of steps:**
  1. the cutover;
  2. the one-week storage pilot;
  3. the record that sets the probe intervals and `book.full_snapshot_interval_s`;
  4. the gate.

  The gate tests the collector as it will run.
- **The days:** two consecutive days with full NBA slates, of at least six games each. A third day of games follows, since day 2's evening faults can reach it.
- **The host and data:** the A1 host, with the Production key and archive. The recorder runs `config.gate.toml` (Consequences) through the relay unit, `raw-recorder-relay.service`.
  - **The gaps are real.** Injected gaps are permanent collection gaps in the Production archive, and closes inside them stay unscoreable. That is the cost of testing on the real feed.
- **The start, `T0`:** the moment the recorder restarts on the gate configuration. Restart away from any dense window, and record `T0` in UTC. It is the gate's `--from`.

### 2. The schedule

Times are US Eastern, standard time after 2026-11-01 (UTC−5). Two days, each with the same eight faults, for 16 in all:

| When (each day) | Fault | Target | Why there |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10:00 | `socket-kill` (day 1 all channels, day 2 `--channel book`) | every subscribed market | before any later game's window opens, so it touches only today's events |
| 10:45 | `process-kill` | every subscribed market | the same; wait until `status` shows every channel connected and resynced before the next fault |
| 15:00 | `sequence-skip --timeout 1800` | a market of tonight's earliest game with recent deltas | outside the dense window: the slow cadence must expose it |
| 15:45 | `channel-stall --seconds 300` | the same kind of market | outside the dense window: the next quiet probe exposes it, up to 300 s later |
| start − 9 min | `sequence-skip` | a market of the game starting | inside a close window (between the largest cutoff, 600 s, and the start) |
| start − 6 min | `channel-stall --seconds 60` | a market of the same game | inside the close window |
| start − 3 min | `resubscribe` | a market of the same game | inside the close window |
| start + 20 min | `resubscribe` | a market of a game in its dense window | after the start, still dense |

**Check the morning faults first.** Before 10:00, confirm in `raw-recorder games` that no event after today's has its window open yet. A weekend matinee tomorrow (from about 12:00) opens its window around 11:00 today, so on such a day move the two whole-connection faults earlier.

That is 2 socket kills, 2 process kills, 4 skips, 4 stalls and 4 resubscribes. Each kind appears at least twice, and skips, stalls and resubscribes fall inside close windows. "The game starting" is the earliest of the evening, so the close-window faults touch as few later windows as possible.

**Spacing.**
- **At least 30 minutes apart,** except the close-window group.
- **The close-window group uses one game,** with different markets where it has more than one. Otherwise each fault waits until the previous fault's gap has closed (`status`: sequences contiguous, no resync pending).
- **No fault on a scope whose gap from an earlier fault is still open,** so each gap has one cause.

**A fault not applied** (a skip that found no delta, a stall that dropped nothing) is listed and not counted. Repeat it within the same hour on a busier market, so each kind keeps its count.

**Unplanned events** (a Novig disconnect, a host reboot) are not faults. Don't stop the run for one: its gaps must still be correct, and they are reported (section 4).

### 3. When the run ends

1. **Injection ends** 48 hours after `T0`. Nothing is injected after that.
2. **Recording continues** until the latest `keep_recording_until_utc` among the faults' reports. Write down that time in UTC: it is the gate's `--to`. It is after the last fault's result.
3. **Then the gate configuration is turned off.** Restore `config.toml`, stop the relay, and restart the recorder between dense windows. Recording itself never stops.
4. **The mirror catches up,** `clv ingest --league-events` loads every touched event, and `clv gate a1 --from T0 --to <end> --min-per-kind 2` runs on the workstation.
5. **The pilot report** over the same period runs once every overlapping event is ingested. It exits 1 until then.

### 4. What passes

The gate passes when all of the following hold:
- **`clv gate a1` prints PASS** for the run. Each fault's checks follow `clv.gate.a1`:
  - **Coverage and reason:** every market and channel a fault touched has a gap covering it, with a reason that fault can cause (`ALLOWED`).
  - **Where it opens:** the gap opens at the last trustworthy observation before the fault. For a dropped delta, that is the last one before its seq, within `gate_a1.receive_slack_ms`.
  - **Where it closes:** at a snapshot received after the fault. On the connection that lost the delta, the snapshot is at or past the dropped seq.
  - **A resubscribe** shows a fresh subscribe snapshot within `gate_a1.resubscribe_wait_s`, and no gap.
  - **Run-wide:** every closed stream gap closes at a snapshot, and a sequence gap closed on its own connection closes past where it opened.
  - **Counts:** every label has a result, and each kind is applied at least twice.
- **Every unattributed gap is explained** in the results section, with its evidence: the recorder's log, the close code, the host's journal. An unexplained gap fails the gate, whatever the tool printed.
- **The recording was continuous.** From `T0` to the end, sessions change only at the scheduled process kills and at explained outages, and no capture window lost data outside the gaps listed.
- **Storage and depth are reported:** the gate's own sections, with the pilot report over the same period beside them. The gate sets no threshold on them; they feed the follow-up records.

**If it fails:**
1. Find the cause, whether in the collector, the replay or the harness.
2. Fix it in a PR with a regression test.
3. Rerun the whole 48 hours. A partial rerun gets no credit.

### 5. The results

A results section is appended to this record after the run. It has:
- `T0` and the end;
- the fault table, with each fault's time, target and outcome;
- the gate's verdict and any failing checks;
- every unattributed gap with its explanation;
- storage per day;
- the depth and pilot summaries.

**No vendor data.** The results quote counts, times, market IDs and verdicts, never prices or raw frames (`docs/decisions/2026-10-14-b1-reference-import.md`). The full reports stay in `data/`.

## Consequences

**Before the run, a small PR:**
- **`tools/raw_recorder/config.gate.toml`** is `config.toml` with `[faults] enabled = true` and `[novig] ws_url` pointing at the relay (8765). A test holds it equal to `config.toml` otherwise, as `config.paper-rehearsal.toml` is held to the Paper config.
- **A drop-in for `raw-recorder.service`** runs the Pi unit on that config for the run. Removing it restores Production. The runbook gets the gate run's steps.
- **A channel stall that drops nothing** is reported as not applied (`ok: false`, with the reason), as a skip that finds no delta already is. Today it reports success, and the gate then fails it because it touched no market.

**The rehearsal confirms the procedure.**
- It checks the provisional `gate_a1.receive_slack_ms` (1,000) and `gate_a1.resubscribe_wait_s` (60) against measured receive latency and resubscribe replies. If they need to change, a §10 revision comes first.
- If the rehearsal shows that a step of this schedule doesn't work as written (for example, a process kill's restart takes longer than the spacing allows), this record is revised before it is accepted.

**The provisional §10 values the gate exercises** (the probe intervals and the dense window) are set by the pilot's follow-up record before the run, not changed during it.
